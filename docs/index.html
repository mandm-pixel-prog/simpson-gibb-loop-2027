Simpson & Gibb Loop

Across the Simpson Desert to Birdsville, north to Lawn Hill, the Savannah Way and Kakadu to Darwin, the Gibb River Road to Broome and the Dampier Peninsula, down the west coast to Perth, then home across the Nullarbor. Back roads where they exist, 200–400 km on travel days, and a night or two wherever it’s worth stopping.

Camping when remote, caravan parks in towns. Facts were checked against park, road and tourism sources on 30 September 2026; recheck closures in May 2027.

On the road

Safety, rules & road reports

Remote travel here is very doable with preparation. The main risks are breakdowns far from help, fuel, heat, crocodiles and wildlife on the road at dusk.

Emergencies

  • Call 000, or 112 from a mobile. Install the Emergency+ app, which shows your coordinates.
  • Carry a PLB and register it free with AMSA at beacons.amsa.gov.au. Keep your contacts and trip details current.
  • A satellite messenger or phone is worth having. Telstra’s satellite SMS can’t call 000.
  • If you break down, stay with the vehicle. It’s far easier to find than a person. In heat, plan on about 1 L of water per person per hour.
  • Leave your plan with someone at home and check in on set days.

Crocodiles (NT and Kimberley)

  • Assume saltwater crocodiles are in any water. Swim only where it’s officially allowed.
  • Camp at least 50 m from water and 2 m above the high-water mark.
  • Clean fish and do chores well back from the edge. Don’t fetch water from the bank.
  • Never wade a crossing such as the Pentecost River, or Cahills Crossing.
  • Windjana Gorge and Tunnel Creek have freshwater crocodiles, which are shy but can bite.

Driving remote roads

  • Avoid driving from dusk to dawn: kangaroos, cattle and camels.
  • Lower tyre pressures for corrugations and sand, and pump back up for bitumen. Pressures depend on your vehicle and load. Francois Peron asks for about 20 psi.
  • Road trains can be 53.5 m long. Pull well off on gravel roads and wait for the dust to clear.
  • Check the road report the night before every unsealed day. Roads close after rain, and you can be fined for driving a closed road.

Fine print

Things that changed in 2026

Floods, a cyclone and closures reshaped parts of this route in 2026. The plan works around them, but every one is worth rechecking before you leave.

  • Cape Leveque: Kooljaman has been closed since November 2021 and the tip is private Native Title land. The plan stays at Djarindjin and Middle Lagoon instead.
  • Flinders Ranges: Bunyeroo Gorge was listed impassable and Brachina Gorge 4WD-only (no towing) in September 2026.
  • Simpson Desert: the QLD closure ran to 31 August 2026 and the QAA Line crossing of Eyre Creek was still shut, with a bypass in use. Normal closure is 1 December to 15 March.
  • Birdsville Track was closed in September 2026. It isn’t on this route, but it’s the usual escape route from Birdsville.
  • Boodjamulla (Lawn Hill) reopened in July 2025 after the 2023 floods. Only 18 campsites; book early.
  • Kakadu: Jim Jim and Twin Falls were closed all of 2026 (reopening planned for 2027). Gunlom was high-clearance 4WD only, with the top pools open.
  • Savannah Way fuel: the Roper Bar store had no fuel for months in 2026. Plan for about 570 km from Borroloola to Mataranka.
  • Gibb River Road: Imintji fuel was closed in 2026. Mt Barnett is the fuel stop between Drysdale River and Derby.
  • Ningaloo: Cyclone Narelle closed Nyinggulara NP (the old Ningaloo Station) and some 14 Mile sites.
  • “Lorne Hill” in the original brief is read here as Lawn Hill, in Boodjamulla National Park.
